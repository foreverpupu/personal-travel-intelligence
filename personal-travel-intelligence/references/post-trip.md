# Post-trip — Reflect & Publish (v1.0)

## Intent
Turn real trip material into a useful, personal, shareable routebook. Never write an ideal itinerary disguised as the actual trip.

## Input precedence and attribution
1. Direct user corrections and contemporaneous firsthand reports, with date and origin when available.
2. Primary artifacts: bookings, emails, photos/captions, actual travel logs and official operators' instructions. Treat photos as visual evidence only; do not invent EXIF/date/location.
3. Reconstructed ledgers and final drafts: useful secondary syntheses, not independent validation.
4. Outside research: supporting geography or current access rules, visibly distinct from the user's historical experience.

If sources differ, keep the discrepancy visible and ask only the question that materially affects the publication. No unsourced causal claims. Keep any synthetic QA examples OUT of the travel history.

## Seven-step workflow
1. **Gather**: discover trip-state / events / decisions / sources and originals; inventory coverage and gaps. Do not pretend to have read missing files.
2. **Reconcile**: extract a dated Actual Journey table: planned, actual, evidence status, attribution, and notable divergence. Booked is not executed. No invented arrival/departure timings.
3. **Select**: separate memorable first-person experiences, useful choices, practical warnings, and optional detail. Keep one or two powerful scenes per day; do not copy the entire itinerary into narrative.
4. **Synthesize lessons**: frame transferable advice as conditional (who it suits, benefit, trade-off, and what to recheck). Preserve firsthand preference rather than declaring a universal rule.
5. **Compose**: lead with lived experience, then explain what made it valuable; follow with precise practical choices. Bilingual Chinese/English navigable places on first mention; brand names in official style. Avoid random untranslated English terms.
6. **Publish**: produce friend edition (concise, decision-relevant) and, only if useful, personal archive edition (more rich feelings, costs, notes). DOCX first, export to PDF, do visual and text-search QA.
7. **Carry forward**: extract a tiny `lessons-learned.md`: experience, applicable conditions, evidence, confidence; never impose one trip's travel party, budget or nights on the next.

## Default chapter structure
- Title + dates + geographic context
- 150–300 word narrative of what *actually* happened
- Key choices: why this stop/route was worth it, and its costs
- 3–5 highly actionable notes: weather, reservations, transport, alternatives
- Source notes / uncertainties (editorial review edition only)

## Hard gates
- Don't publish a fabricated visit, booking, wildlife sighting, closure reason or rank.
- Don't say an operator canceled merely because weather was bad.
- Historical operating hours/phone numbers/awards are dated, not guaranteed current.
- Never equate observing a sight from public space with entering a paid or restricted attraction.
- Need identifiable photos? Ask the user for captions or documented metadata; do not guess.
- For Word/PDF: render every page and visually examine; inspect Chinese text encoding and searchable labels, especially place names and tables; avoid misleading compatibility radicals.
- If the requested files cannot be generated, supply the complete draft and say what was not produced.

## Minimal acceptable handoff
`actual-journey.md` or dated source ledger + routebook.docx + routebook.pdf + `lessons-learned.md` + short QA note. For small chapters, one compact source note inside the file can replace an extensive ledger.
